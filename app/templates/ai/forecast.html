How to read this page
Every number explained in plain language

How the forecast works: the model studies the last 180 days of stock movement for the selected product and projects the next N days. Prophet finds weekly/monthly/yearly seasonal patterns, XGBoost learns from lag and calendar features, and Ensemble averages both for a steadier prediction.

Baseline — the average units moved per day over the period. Think of it as a "normal day".

Predicted — the total units the model expects to move over the horizon you picked.

Delta — how much recent demand differs from the baseline, in %. Positive = demand is picking up, negative = slowing down.

Accuracy — how close the model's typical prediction is to what actually happened. 100% = perfect, above 60% is dependable.

Predicted demand (card) — sum of every forecast day across the horizon.

Confidence band — the likely range around the predictions (± units). A narrow band means the model is certain; a wide band means demand is unpredictable.

MAE (Mean Absolute Error) — on an average day, the prediction is off by this many units. Lower is better.

MAPE (Mean Absolute % Error) — the same as MAE, but as a percentage of actual demand, so it compares fairly across products.

RMSE (Root Mean Square Error) — like MAE but punishes big misses harder. If RMSE is much larger than MAE, a few days were badly wrong.

Observations — how many days of real data were used to score the model.

WAPE (Weighted Absolute Percentage Error) — sum of absolute errors divided by sum of actuals. Better for intermittent demand than MAPE.

MASE (Mean Absolute Scaled Error) — MAE relative to a seasonal naive forecast. < 1 means better than naive.

Interval Coverage — % of actuals falling within the 95% prediction interval. Should be ~95%.

Backtest — rolling-origin evaluation on chronologically held-out data. The gold standard for forecast validation.

Portfolio table — one row per product: what a normal day looks like (Baseline), what the next period will need (Predicted), whether demand is rising or falling (Delta), how much to trust the number (Accuracy), and which model was actually used.
